## What Plantar Fasciitis Actually Is

The plantar fascia is a thick band of connective tissue running from the heel bone (calcaneus) to the base of the toes, supporting the arch of the foot. PF is an overuse injury at the fascia's attachment to the heel — micro-tears that don't fully heal between training sessions.

**Hallmark symptoms:**
- Sharp/stabbing pain in the heel on first steps in the morning
- Pain that eases after warming up but returns after rest or long sessions
- Tenderness on direct pressure over the medial heel attachment
- Worsens with bare feet on hard surfaces, hills, worn shoes

**Why it happens — common triggers:**
- Worn-out shoes with collapsed midsole support
- Sudden volume or intensity increase
- Tight calves and Achilles (transmits load to fascia)
- Hard training surfaces (concrete, treadmill on max settings)
- Weak intrinsic foot muscles
- Inadequate warmup (cold fascia under sudden load)

**Most likely culprits in this case:** worn-out shoes (replacement underway) plus sudden Apr training intensification + the 18 Apr TT effort on inadequate warmup.

## Daily Prehab Protocol — Non-Negotiable

Do all of these every day, regardless of whether you've trained.

### Morning — before first step out of bed

1. **Plantar fascia stretch (Digiovanni protocol)** — sit on edge of bed. Cross right foot over left knee. Pull toes back into dorsiflexion until you feel a stretch through the arch. **Hold 30 sec × 3 reps.** *Most important single intervention — does more than anything else.*
2. **Calf stretch in bed** — towel around forefoot, pull toes toward you with knee straight. 30 sec × 2.
3. *Then* take your first steps.

### During the day

4. **Frozen water bottle roll** — fill a 500 ml bottle with water and freeze. Roll under arch, 10 min. Twice daily and after any session (cycling, running, weights).
5. **Calf + soleus stretch** — wall stretch, knee straight (gastroc) 45 sec × 2; then knee bent (soleus) 45 sec × 2. Three times daily.
6. **Towel scrunches** — sit, towel on floor, scrunch with toes. 2 min, twice daily.
7. **Toe spreads** — spread toes wide, hold 5 sec, release. 15 reps, twice daily.

### Evening

8. **Eccentric heel drops** — off a step. Both feet up, drop slowly to one foot (3 sec down) below step level, both feet back up. **3 × 15 each leg, once daily.** Skip if it provokes heel pain. Add load (backpack with books) once pain-free.
9. **Self-massage** — thumbs into the arch, golf-ball or lacrosse-ball roll, 5 min. Find tight spots and dwell.

### Weekly

- **Single-leg balance** — barefoot on towel, eyes closed, 60 sec × 3 each leg. Builds intrinsic foot strength.

## Shockwave Therapy Notes

- Shockwave (ESWT) is well-evidenced for sub-acute and chronic PF — typically 3–5 sessions, 1 week apart.
- Mild soreness for 24–48 hr after each session is normal.
- **Avoid heavy loading 24 hr post-session.** Schedule cycling sessions *before* the appointment, not after, on shockwave days.
- Tell physio if pain worsens beyond 48 hr — may need protocol adjustment.

## Shoe Replacement Guidance

### What to look for (PF-friendly)

- **Heel-toe drop 8–12 mm** — reduces fascia tension under load. *Avoid 0–4 mm drop until fully cleared.*
- **Structured midsole** — some medial-side support if you tend to overpronate.
- **Plenty of cushion** under the arch and heel.
- **Stable platform** — not so soft that it lets the arch collapse.

### What to avoid (until cleared)

- Zero-drop / minimalist (Altra, Vivobarefoot, Merrell barefoot)
- Carbon-plated racers as a daily trainer (fine to keep purely for race day later)
- Anything where the heel feels firm or hollow when pressed
- Old shoes — once the midsole creases when pressed sideways, they're done

### Shortlist (SA market, Apr 2026)

**Stability + cushion (gold standard for PF):**
- Asics Gel-Kayano (most recommended for recovering PF runners)
- Brooks Adrenaline GTS or Glycerin GTS
- Saucony Guide (lighter than Kayano)

**Maximalist cushion:**
- Hoka Bondi (max underfoot)
- Hoka Clifton (lighter alternative)

### Buying tips

1. **Get fitted properly.** Sweat Shop, Run-Ways, or Totalsports Running. Bring the old pair so staff can read the wear pattern.
2. **Try at end of day** — feet are slightly swollen, closer to mid-run state.
3. **Walk and jog in-store** — most shops have a treadmill.
4. **Consider a rotation pair.** Alternating two pairs reduces repetitive-strain injury rate by ~40% in the literature.
5. **Replace by ~600 km.** Track in Garmin Connect or Strava.

## Cross-Training While Off Running

Goal: maintain aerobic fitness without loading the fascia.

### Bike (primary)

- 4–5 sessions/week, 45–90 min each
- Mix: 1 long ride, 2 easy aerobic, 1 interval, 1 tempo
- Cycling HR runs ~10 bpm lower than running for equivalent effort — go by RPE, not running zones
- Both indoor (controlled, no road risk) and outdoor (mental refresh) are valid

### Bike interval session template

15 min WU @ HR 130 → 5 × 4 min hard (HR 165–175, RPE 8) / 2 min easy → 10 min CD. ~50 min total.

This holds VO2max well — research shows ~80–90% running fitness retention via cycling for 3–4 weeks if intensity is preserved.

### What NOT to substitute

- **Stairmaster / hill walking on incline** — repetitive heel/calf load. Avoid.
- **Pool running / aqua jogging** — fine, but technically demanding and pool access dependent
- **Elliptical** — OK as backup, but mimics impact more than bike does
- **Rowing** — fine for cardio, keep volume modest (hip flexor demand)

### Strength work (during off-period)

- Upper body + core: keep current routine
- Lower body: avoid heavy posterior chain (deadlifts, calf raises into pain). Bodyweight squats, single-leg work, glute bridges fine.

| Day 6 | Long ride 60–75 min | — |
| Day 7 | Rest |  |

Then add 1 km per week to longest run, hold easy pace, no quality work for 2 weeks minimum. Reintroduce tempo only when 5 km easy is fully pain-free.

## Stop Signs — Any One Means Stop Running

1. Morning first-step pain >3/10 on a 0–10 scale
2. Pain that increases during a run (vs. easing as you warm up)
3. Heel pain still present 2+ hours after a run
4. Limping mid-run or having to alter gait
5. Pain on direct palpation of the medial heel attachment
6. Pain triggered by single-leg hop test (don't routinely test — only if symptoms persist)

If any of these trigger, return to bike-only and call physio.

## What Makes PF Become Chronic

These patterns turn 2 weeks off into 6 months off. Avoid all of them.

1. **Running through pain** because it "feels okay once warm" — the warming masks tissue stress, not damage.
2. **Returning to full intensity on day one** of clearance — the tissue is still healing.
3. **Skipping prehab** once symptoms ease — relapse rate is 50%+ without sustained loading work.
4. **Not replacing the shoes** that contributed to the problem.
5. **Ignoring tight calves** — they're upstream of the problem. Stretch + foam roll daily forever.
6. **Going back to hills too soon** — uphill running maximises fascia tension.
